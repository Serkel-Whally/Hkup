Comprehensive Plan: Cellulite Business
1. Product vision
Cellulite Business will be a mobile-first business-management web app for Ghanaian microbusinesses that sell through WhatsApp, Instagram, TikTok, physical shops, and personal networks.
The platform will help business owners:
• Record sales and expenses
• Track inventory
• Monitor customer debt
• Generate digital receipts
• Understand profit
• Share products and payment reminders through WhatsApp
• Manage Mobile Money payment references
• Operate a simple online storefront
The first target market should be data resellers and small online sellers. This lets you reuse the Cellulite brand and your existing data-selling concept while building something with recurring subscription revenue.
Ghana is a strong environment for a mobile-first business tool. Bank of Ghana data shows substantial Mobile Money usage, and its national payments strategy reports financial inclusion of 81% based on the 2024 Global Findex. (Bureau of Ghanian Affairs)

───

2. Core problem
Many small sellers currently use a combination of:
• WhatsApp chats
• Mobile Money messages
• Paper notebooks
• Phone calculators
• Excel sheets
• Screenshots
• Memory
This creates several problems:
1. Sellers cannot calculate their true profit.
2. Stock quantities become inaccurate.
3. Customer debts are forgotten.
4. Payment screenshots are difficult to reconcile.
5. Personal and business transactions become mixed.
6. Sellers repeatedly type product and payment information.
7. Records can disappear when a phone is lost.
8. Owners cannot supervise staff effectively.
9. Customers do not receive professional receipts.
10. Sellers do not know which products perform best.
Cellulite Business should replace this fragmented workflow with one simple system.

11. Validation plan
Do not spend months building before speaking with customers.
Interview 20 potential users
Speak with:
• 10 data resellers
• 5 fashion sellers
• 5 other WhatsApp-based businesses
Ask them to show you how they currently:
• Receive orders
• Confirm payments
• Record sales
• Calculate profit
• Track customer debts
• Manage stock
• Issue receipts
Avoid asking only, “Would you use this?” People often say yes without paying.
Ask:
• What happened the last time an order was forgotten?
• How do you calculate today’s profit?
• How do you know whether a customer has paid?
• What records do you keep?
• Which part takes the most time?
• Have you paid for software before?
• Would you pay GH₵25 monthly to solve this?
• What would prevent you from using it?
Validation threshold
Proceed confidently when:
• At least 10 interviewees have the same serious problem
• At least five agree to test the product
• At least three are willing to pay before the product is perfect
Your first users should help shape the product.

14. Multi-tenant design
Because many businesses will use the same platform, every business-owned record must include a business_id.
Example:
text
Business A
  ├── Products
  ├── Customers
  ├── Orders
  └── Expenses

Business B
  ├── Products
  ├── Customers
  ├── Orders
  └── Expenses

Every database query must verify that the signed-in user belongs to the requested business.
Never rely only on a business ID sent by the frontend. The server must enforce ownership.

16. Payment strategy
MVP approach
During the first MVP:
• Let sellers record payment manually
• Allow them to add a Mobile Money transaction reference
• Do not hold their customers’ funds
• Use a payment provider only for Cellulite subscription payments
Later approach
Integrate a Bank of Ghana-approved payment service provider for:
• Subscription payments
• Customer checkout
• Payment confirmation
• Webhooks
• Refund workflows
The Bank of Ghana publishes approved electronic-money issuers and payment-service providers. Use an approved provider and confirm its exact licence coverage before integration. (Bureau of Ghanian Affairs)
Important distinction
There is a major difference between:
1. Helping sellers record payments
2. Collecting payment through a licensed provider
3. Holding customer funds in a platform wallet
The third option creates significantly greater regulatory and security obligations. Avoid operating a stored-value wallet unless you have proper legal and regulatory guidance.

17. Data-bundle integration strategy
Do not make automated bundle delivery a requirement for the first launch.
Stage 1: Manual fulfillment
The seller:
• Receives the order
• Confirms payment
• Processes it through the existing supplier panel
• Marks the order completed
This lets you validate demand before relying on an unstable third-party integration.
Stage 2: Single-provider integration
Connect one reliable supplier that provides:
• Documented API
• Product list
• Order endpoint
• Status endpoint
• Balance endpoint
• Unique transaction references
• Webhook or polling support
• Clear refund rules
Stage 3: Multiple providers
Create a provider adapter:
text
DataProvider
 ├── getProducts()
 ├── checkBalance()
 ├── placeOrder()
 ├── checkOrderStatus()
 └── cancelOrder()

Then implement:
text
ProviderAAdapter
ProviderBAdapter
ProviderCAdapter

This prevents your entire application from depending on one supplier’s API format.
Fulfillment rules
• Never mark an order successful before provider confirmation.
• Give every provider request an idempotency key.
• Prevent duplicate fulfillment.
• Store raw provider responses securely.
• Retry only safe operations.
• Flag uncertain orders for manual review.
• Reconcile provider balances daily.

18. Security plan
Security is essential because the platform handles customer details, business records and payment references.
Authentication
• Hash passwords using Argon2id or bcrypt
• Use secure, HTTP-only cookies
• Add login rate limiting
• Support password reset tokens with expiration
• Invalidate active sessions after password changes
• Add two-factor authentication for owners later
Authorization
• Enforce business ownership server-side
• Use role-based permissions
• Never trust hidden frontend controls
• Test cross-business access attempts
• Restrict exports and settings to authorized roles
Application security
• Validate all inputs
• Sanitize uploaded filenames
• Restrict file type and size
• Use parameterized database queries
• Protect against CSRF where required
• Set secure headers
• Use HTTPS only
• Rate-limit sensitive endpoints
• Protect webhook endpoints with signatures
• Avoid exposing stack traces
Data security
• Encrypt data in transit
• Use encryption at rest through the hosting provider
• Back up the database
• Test database restoration
• Keep production credentials out of source code
• Rotate secrets
• Separate development and production environments
Financial integrity
• Use immutable transaction entries where practical
• Do not overwrite completed-payment history
• Record refunds as separate events
• Add audit logs
• Store provider references
• Make webhook processing idempotent
• Reconcile payments regularly

19. Privacy and compliance
The app will process names, phone numbers, customer transactions and possibly uploaded receipts.
Ghana’s Data Protection Commission states that organisations collecting or processing personal data must register and comply with the Data Protection Act, 2012. Its guidance emphasizes principles including transparency, data minimisation, purpose limitation and accountability. (Data Protection Ghana)
Before public launch:
• Register the business properly
• Determine your Data Protection Commission obligations
• Publish a privacy policy
• Publish terms of service
• Create a data-retention policy
• Collect only necessary information
• Provide account deletion procedures
• Allow correction of inaccurate data
• Document third-party processors
• Sign appropriate agreements with vendors
• Establish a security-incident process
• Obtain legal and tax advice for the final business model
Do not copy another company’s privacy policy without adapting it to your actual practices.

30. Risk register
Risk: Users do not record every sale
Mitigation:
• Make sales entry extremely fast
• Add repeat-sale shortcuts
• Show immediate daily profit
• Send optional reminders
• Offer storefront-generated orders
Risk: Sellers refuse monthly subscriptions
Mitigation:
• Validate willingness to pay early
• Offer annual plans
• Demonstrate money saved or recovered
• Provide setup services
• Target businesses with regular transaction volume
Risk: Third-party data API fails
Mitigation:
• Launch with manual fulfillment
• Use provider adapters
• Add manual override
• Maintain provider-status logs
• Support more than one provider later
Risk: Payment disputes
Mitigation:
• Store references
• Keep status history
• Show clear payment states
• Provide manual review
• Avoid holding seller funds initially
Risk: Competitors copy features
Mitigation:
• Focus on niche workflows
• Provide strong onboarding
• Develop trusted reseller relationships
• Improve reliability
• Build local support and brand recognition
Risk: Solo-developer overload
Mitigation:
• Keep the MVP narrow
• Use managed services
• Automate deployment
• Write support documentation
• Avoid custom features for individual customers
• Schedule feature releases rather than accepting every request

31. Solo-developer operating rules
Follow these rules strictly:
1. Build one core workflow before adding more modules.
2. Do not create native mobile apps initially.
3. Do not build a wallet initially.
4. Do not support every business category at launch.
5. Charge early.
6. Speak to users every week.
7. Fix reliability before adding impressive features.
8. Automate backups and deployments.
9. Keep third-party integrations replaceable.
10. Review revenue and retention every month.

Final product statement
Cellulite Business is a Ghana-focused mobile business manager that helps data resellers and WhatsApp sellers record orders, track payments, manage stock, follow customer debts and understand profit.
The strongest execution strategy is to launch it first as a manual business-management system for data resellers, acquire paying users, and add automated data delivery only after the core workflow proves valuable.
